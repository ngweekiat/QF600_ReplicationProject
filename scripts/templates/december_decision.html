December 2026 Fed decision: what it means for the strategy

Markets move on the surprise in a decision, not the decision itself. If a hike of size h is priced with probability p, a hike surprises by +(1 − p)·h and a hold by −p·h. This page maps each surprise through the 2-year Treasury yield into the final strategy's one-month active return and its benchmark's return.

How to read this

  • Expected values are linear in the surprise, so the probability-weighted expected effect is zero by construction. The decision changes the spread of outcomes, not their average.
  • The 90% range adds everything other than rates: residual factor risk, stock-specific risk and the estimation error of the rate betas.
  • Rate betas come from monthly changes in the 2-year yield, which mix policy news and growth news. The index's sensitivity changes sign between the two windows for that reason.
  • Each surprise is assumed to move the 2-year yield one-for-one; a smaller pass-through scales every expected value down in proportion.
  • Holdings are the strategy's last book in the data. This is a scenario range for a frozen strategy, not a forecast of returns or of the Fed.
